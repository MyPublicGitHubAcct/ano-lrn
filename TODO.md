### Add GENDYN stochastic synthesis

Add `gendyn(fs, duration, num_segments, amp_walk_step, dur_walk_step, amp_dist, dur_dist, seed)` to `src/python/generators/` as a new `stochastic.py` submodule. GENDYN (Xenakis, 1991) generates audio by concatenating short linear segments whose amplitudes and durations evolve via independent constrained random walks: on each new segment, `amp[k+1] = clip(amp[k] + amp_walk_step · r_a, −1, 1)` and `dur[k+1] = clip(dur[k] + dur_walk_step · r_d, dur_min, dur_max)`, where `r_a` and `r_d` are random deviates drawn from `amp_dist` and `dur_dist` respectively (supported distributions: `"uniform"`, `"cauchy"`, `"logistic"`; Xenakis used Cauchy for sharp, dissonant textures). Add `docs/generators/stochastic.md` explaining the constrained random walk and the probability distributions Xenakis used in his compositions. Add `notebooks/generators_stochastic.ipynb` demonstrating the four patches. GENDYN is one of the few synthesis methods that produces genuinely non-periodic, non-noise signals and maps well to a VCV Rack probabilistic oscillator module.

### Add wave digital filter diode clipper

Wave Digital Filters (Fettweis, 1986) simulate analogue circuits by converting each component into a port described by incident (`a`) and reflected (`b`) wave variables related by `b = Γ(a)`, where `Γ` is a linear (resistor/capacitor/inductor) or nonlinear (diode) scattering relation. Add `notebooks/virtual_analog_wdf.ipynb` demonstrating the diode clipper alongside `soft_clip` and `wavefold`. WDF simulation is the only method in this codebase that derives clipping character directly from physical component values, making it the most accurate path to VCV Rack analogue distortion emulation.

### Add instantaneous frequency and AM-FM decomposition

Add `amfm_decompose(signal, fs)` to `src/python/time_frequency/amfm.py` as a new submodule. AM-FM decomposition recovers the instantaneous amplitude envelope `a(t)` and instantaneous frequency `f(t)` from a narrowband signal using the analytic signal: compute the analytic signal `z(t) = signal + j · H{signal}` via the Hilbert transform (`scipy.signal.hilbert`), then `a(t) = |z(t)|` and `phi(t) = angle(z(t))`. Add `plot_time_frequency_amfm.py` showing instantaneous frequency vs. time for a chirp alongside the STFT spectrogram for comparison. Add `docs/time_frequency/amfm.md` deriving the analytic signal, the Bedrosian theorem (conditions for valid AM-FM separation), and the phase unwrapping step. Add `notebooks/time_frequency_amfm.ipynb` demonstrating AM-FM decomposition on a chirp, a vibrato sine, and a broadband signal through the filterbank. AM-FM decomposition is the theoretical basis for instantaneous pitch detection, vibrato measurement, and formant tracking in natural sounds — applications that are otherwise obscured by the short-time Fourier frame boundary artefacts visible in the STFT.

### Add acoustic Doppler shift simulation

The Doppler effect shifts the apparent frequency of a moving sound source: when source and observer approach each other the received frequency is `f_r = f_s · (sound_speed + v_o) / (sound_speed − v_s)` where `v_s` and `v_o` are signed velocities along the source-observer axis. A positive `direction` means approaching (frequency rises), negative means receding (frequency falls). Add `docs/spatial/doppler.py.md` deriving the sample-by-sample delay update from the classical Doppler formula. Add `notebooks/spatial_doppler.ipynb` demonstrating a flyby with time-varying velocity. Doppler shift is one of the few purely physical spatial effects that VCV Rack modules almost never implement; it is a useful building block for motion-simulated reverb tails and spatial sound design.
